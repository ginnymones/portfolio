# CHALLENGE (what was going on and what I set out to do)
challenge: |-
  Possibly the most difficult part of participating in the 100 days of UI challenge is actually starting — and starting off strong, at that. “Strong” meant something I could enjoy making and also use to stretch my abilities further. As it turned out, after careful consideration, “strong” was an imaginary astrology app for people like me who love studying said topic.

  "Why an app where I can find my zodiac readings, though? Can't this just be a website?" It can be, but that doesn't mean we should limit ourselves to just making readings available on a website. I meant the app to be an accompaniment to the web experience, especially for those who prefer to maintain separate apps for different interests or those who simply have too many tabs open in their browser. Putting the readings in a mobile app also makes the experience more personal — something that consumers of zodiac or astrology readings usually enjoy.
---

My approach for this first challenge has been my go-to for nearly all design decisions I've had to make. I asked myself the following things:

1. **What is already working?** — Daily, weekly, monthly forecasts. Users love those. Surprisingly, email notifications of said forecasts are also still effective, so reaching users through their inbox could be a viable option. People don't mind making a few extra taps to see content that is tailor-made for them.
2. **What is not working?** — Jittery-looking page loading between the time-span views. Cluttered website layouts are also a huge problem (and a trend, unfortunately).
3. **What else are my users interested in?** — This is important as this will help position my app as something that provides more value than the rest.

***

![Desktop layout](/images/case-studies/100-days-of-ui-sign-up-header.jpg)

As the goal was to design the sign up page and nothing else, I needed to communicate the whole point of the app right from the get-go. That meant an all-encompassing copy (Start your stellar journey), striking visuals, and strong but "mystical" colors — if that makes sense.

I played around with Figma agents and whipped up some quick screens for the home, daily reading, and daily tarot pages. While AI was able to put these pages together quickly, it was not able to get the nuances of the design language that I had put together so I had to make big changes to the design, specifically on the icons used and font choices.
